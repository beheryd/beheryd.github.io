---
title: How should we be thinking about overthinking?
---
A random shower thought: Intuitively, I think that the only downside to overthinking is the cost of doing so. So then the idea would be something like, “Yeah, you might arrive at a solution, but there are diminishing returns in the quality of the solution, so it’s not worth spending more compute if you’ll end up with only a slightly better solution.”

But surely overthinking hurts not just one’s ability to arrive at a solution, but also the effectiveness of that solution. I guess, in this sense, a “good” solution would be defined by its ability to (i) actually solve the problem and (ii) solve it efficiently.

With that said, I wonder if anyone has looked into whether overthinking is truly induced just by allocating more compute to a problem, or whether other things are going on in the background.
